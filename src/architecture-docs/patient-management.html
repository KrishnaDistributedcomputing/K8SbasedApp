What This Context Does

Patient Management is the master patient index of the EHR system. When a patient walks into the hospital for the first time, this service captures their identity — name, date of birth, address, phone number, insurance details, emergency contacts, known allergies, and initial vitals.

Every other bounded context in the system depends on Patient Management. The scheduling service needs patient identifiers to book appointments. The pharmacy needs allergy data to check drug interactions. The lab needs patient demographics to label specimens. This makes Patient Management the most referenced and most critical context.

In plain English: Think of this as the hospital's front desk and patient registration system. It answers the question: "Who is this patient, and what do we need to know about them before any clinical work begins?"

Domain Model — Entities & Value Objects

Patient Aggregate Root

The central entity. Every allergy, vital, and emergency contact belongs to a specific patient.

PropertyTypeDescription
PatientIdint (PK)Unique identifier, auto-generated
FirstNamestringLegal first name
LastNamestringLegal last name
DateOfBirthDateTimeUsed for age-based clinical rules
GenderstringM / F / Other / Unknown
EmailstringFor notifications and portal access
PhonestringPrimary contact number

Demographics Value Object

Address, language, ethnicity, marital status. Immutable — replaced when updated.

Insurance Entity

Health plan details — provider name, policy number, group number, coverage dates.

Allergy Entity

Known allergies (drug, food, environmental). Severity: mild, moderate, severe. Critical for pharmacy drug interaction checks.

Vitals Entity

Temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, SpO2. Each reading is timestamped and linked to the recording provider.

EmergencyContact Value Object

Name, phone, relationship to patient. Used when patient cannot make decisions.

Domain Events Published

When something important happens in Patient Management, these events are published to the event bus so other contexts can react:

EventWhen It FiresWho ListensWhat They Do
PatientRegisteredNew patient createdClinical, Scheduling, AuditCreate patient reference in their own stores
PatientUpdatedDemographics changedAll contextsSync updated name/address if cached
AllergyRegisteredNew allergy recordedPharmacy, AuditPharmacy re-checks active prescriptions
CriticalVitalsAlertVitals outside safe rangeClinical, Notification, AuditImmediate alert to the attending clinician

Event Flow Example

Nurse records vitals → SpO2 = 88% (below 90%) → CriticalVitalsAlert published → Notification sends SMS to doctor

Data Store

Current State

Shares ehrdb PostgreSQL database with all other contexts. Tables: patients, allergies, vitals.

Target State

Own dedicated patient-db PostgreSQL database. Migrated via dual-write + CDC during Phase 1 of Strangler Fig migration.
