What This Context Does

Every interaction in a healthcare system is tied to a provider — the doctor, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, or specialist who is responsible for patient care. Provider Management is the master registry that answers: Who are our providers? What are they licensed to do? Where do they work? When are they available?

Each provider has a unique NPI (National Provider Identifier) — a 10-digit number issued by CMS that's used for billing, prescribing, and identity across all healthcare systems. This context stores credentials (board certifications, DEA numbers for prescribing), department assignments, and weekly schedule templates.

In plain English: This is the hospital's staff directory on steroids. Not just names and phone numbers — it knows that Dr. Smith is board-certified in cardiology, licensed in Ontario, works Mondays and Wednesdays in Building A, and her DEA number lets her prescribe controlled substances.

Credential Entity

Board certifications, DEA numbers, and specialty qualifications. Credentials have start/end dates and must be verified against external registries. Expired credentials block scheduling and prescribing.

DepartmentAssignment Entity

Links providers to departments (Cardiology, ICU, Emergency, Family Medicine). A provider can belong to multiple departments with different roles (attending, resident, consulting).

ScheduleTemplate Entity

Weekly recurring availability patterns — "Dr. Smith sees patients Mon/Wed/Fri 9AM-5PM in Clinic B." Used by the Scheduling context to offer appointment slots to patients.

ProviderStatus Value Object

Active, On Leave, Suspended, Retired. When status changes, downstream systems react — suspended providers can't be scheduled, retired providers are archived but never deleted for audit trails.
