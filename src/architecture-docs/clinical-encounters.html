What This Context Does

When a patient sees a doctor, that visit is called an Encounter. Clinical Encounters is responsible for documenting everything that happens during that visit — the reason for the visit, what the doctor observed, what diagnosis was made, what treatment plan was created, and the doctor's clinical notes.

This context uses ICD-10 codes for diagnoses (the international standard for classifying diseases). For example, "E11.9" means Type 2 Diabetes without complications. These codes are critical for insurance billing, clinical research, and public health reporting.

In plain English: This is the doctor's chart during your visit. It records why you came in, what the doctor found, what's wrong (the diagnosis), and what the plan is (medications, follow-ups, procedures). Every visit creates one Encounter.

Domain Model

Encounter Aggregate Root

One encounter = one visit. Links a patient to a provider at a point in time. All medical records, diagnoses, and notes hang off this.

PropertyTypeDescription
EncounterIdint (PK)Unique visit identifier

TypestringOffice Visit, Emergency, Telehealth, Procedure
StatusstringScheduled, InProgress, Completed, Cancelled
ChiefComplaintstringPatient's primary reason for the visit

MedicalRecord Entity

Structured documentation of findings, observations, and assessment during the encounter. Each record has a type (Progress Note, H&P, Discharge Summary).

Diagnosis (ICD-10) Value Object

Standardized diagnosis code. Immutable once recorded. Examples: J06.9 (Upper respiratory infection), M54.5 (Low back pain).

TreatmentPlan Entity

The doctor's plan — medications to prescribe, labs to order, follow-up schedule, lifestyle recommendations, referrals.

ClinicalNotes Entity

Free-text physician notes in SOAP format (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan). The narrative record of the visit.
